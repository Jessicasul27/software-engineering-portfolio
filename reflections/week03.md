1. The reworks triggered in a waterfall model:
* **Requirements:** The requirements already established would have to completely be changed to account for the new feature. This will result in going back to the start of the waterfall model process as it was completed in Month 1. "Requirements were signed off in Month 1".
* **Design:** The design of the project up until that point will have to be reworked as the design was completed and approve in Month 2. Due to the waterfall model following such a strict and tight process, the design will have to be redone. "design completed and approved in Month 2".
* **Implementation:** The implementation that was completed in Month 4 will have to be restarted as the implementation of the design is heavily dependent on the design stage. However, due to the design phase needing to be revisited as the requirements have changed, even though implementation was half-way done, it will have to be revisited and reworked " midway through Month 4 (implementation)".
* **Testing:** Due to testing being in Month 5, it had not yet been in development. However, while the testing stage may not need to be reworked, it definitely needs to be revisited due to the new requirements, new design and new implementation method. " system testing scheduled for Month 5".
* **Maintenance:** The maintenance of the system will also need to be reworked, this includes documentation that had been done up until the new requirement being added.



Overall, the entire project needs to be revisited and reworked. Due to the waterfall model having such a strict and tight model, it does not give room for adaptable change. As a result, each step in the process affects the next. The additional requirement affects the design, the design changes affect the way the system is maintained, tested and the resulting product. This will result in a product being over-budget, late and lacking requirements.

**2.** The reworks triggered in an agile model:

* **Requirements:** The agile method welcomes change. The model prepares for the system to constantly change and actually relies on these changing requirements to enhance and improve the system based on the user stories. Due to the agile method being incremental and iterative, it relies on customer feedback to create the next increment of the system and to enhance it.
* **Design:** Due to the agile method working in increments, the design is done in chunks based on the new set of requirements in the form of user stories. As this model is used to changing requirements, the addition of the requirement to the design would be a lot easier to incorporate. 
* **Implementation:** As agile runs in short fast paced increments, the implementation of the system will not be affected as it is directly working with design constantly updating with the new set of requirements given.
* **Testing:** Testing of the system is done with every design/implementation, which in the agile method case is every 2 weeks. This essentially means the system is tested every time a new feature is added and not all at the end making it very efficient.
* **Maintenance:** The maintenance of the system again will not need to reworked as the agile method is always working with each new addition of the system. Agile is also not document heavy due to the priority being with delivering a tested, well designed working system every two weeks in this case.



Overall, the entire project would continue as usual, treating this new requirement like it does every 2 weeks. It will adapt to the change and welcome it as agile method main drive for design, implementation and testing is dependent on customer feedback, adapting as much as possible. 



**The cost of change:**

The cost and effort of the **waterfall model** would be very expensive and would take a lot of effort to revisit every stage, stages that took teams 1 entire month to do. They would essentially need to go back on months of work in all stages (requirements, design, implementation, maintenance and testing) and rework months of work. 



The cost and effort of the **agile method** would not be expensive and significantly cheaper as the agile method is able to adapt to change and catch these requirement changes earlier. They would not need to go back on months of work due to their two week sprints. Incorporation of changes will be a lot easier and cheaper.
